Senior Capstone · State Estimation

EKF Magnetic Signature Navigation

Case Western Reserve University · advised by Dr. Brian Taylor · MATLAB

A bio-inspired agent navigates a simulated North Atlantic using Earth's magnetic signature — intensity and inclination — the way migratory species are believed to. The complication: secular variation slowly drifts the magnetic field over time, so a fixed location's signature isn't constant.

I built an Extended Kalman Filter that removes the prior assumption of perfect field knowledge. It predicts how each goal's magnetic signature will have evolved by the time the agent arrives, using a motion model of the drift, and then corrects that estimate against the measured signature on arrival, weighted by the Kalman gain. A key debugging win along the way: catching a measurement model that ignored its state input entirely, which was silently zeroing the Kalman gain and making every correction a no-op.

The filter was validated across 250 migration cycles under translation, rotation, and combined field-change scenarios.

Thermofluids (EMAE 355) · Coursework

Geothermal CO₂ Sequestration Power Cycle

Case Western Reserve University · Python, CoolProp

A closed-loop power cycle that uses CO₂ as both the sequestration fluid and the working fluid — circulating it through a 3.2 km saline aquifer at 224°C to drive a turbine, offsetting the pump power needed to keep the loop moving.

I built a quasi-1D well model using Churchill friction factors, Gnielinski/Dittus–Boelter Nusselt correlations, NTU-ε heat transfer through the wellbore, and Darcy's law for aquifer pressure drop — then wrote a 2D optimizer that sweeps injection pressure and circulation mass flow to maximize net work output, with thermodynamically correct P–h diagrams generated from real CO₂ property data via CoolProp.
