About me

I'm a mechanical engineer who treats software as a tool, not an identity. My real interest sits at the hardware–software intersection — designing physical systems, then making them intelligent through machine learning and reinforcement learning.

I finished my B.S. in Mechanical Engineering at Case Western Reserve University, where my senior capstone — advised by Dr. Brian Taylor — built an Extended Kalman Filter for bio-inspired magnetic navigation. This fall I'm starting an M.S. in Mechanical Engineering (Research) at Carnegie Mellon, chosen specifically for its applied, project-driven robotics ecosystem.

Outside coursework, I've served in regional leadership with the National Society of Black Engineers (NSBE) as Assistant Treasurer across Region II and Region IV, and I do independent research into energy systems and manufacturing economics.

Longer-term, I'm working toward a career in robotics R&D — companies like Amazon Robotics and ABB are on my radar, and a PhD remains an open path depending on where the research takes me. My personal build, Project JARVIS, is where I chase that combination directly: an intelligent home robotics system that's actually worth living with.
